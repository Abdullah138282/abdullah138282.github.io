What is a reaction time test?

A reaction time test measures how quickly you respond to a specific signal. In this online reaction test, the screen changes color and you click as soon as you notice the change. Your response is recorded in milliseconds.

Thinkora uses five rounds and reports both your average and best reaction time. The result describes your performance on this particular task rather than measuring overall intelligence or every aspect of reaction ability.

What is a good reaction time?

For simple visual reaction tasks, many people produce results around 200 to 300 milliseconds. However, there is no single reaction time that applies to everyone. Results can vary with the task, device, attention, fatigue, practice and testing conditions.

If you are comparing your own results, using the same device and similar conditions can make the comparison more useful.

Reaction time in milliseconds

Reaction time is commonly reported in milliseconds, written as ms. One second contains 1,000 milliseconds. Therefore, a reaction time of 250 ms represents one quarter of a second.

A lower number means a shorter measured response interval in this test. It does not by itself describe a person's overall cognitive ability.

How the online reaction test works

Each round begins with a waiting period. When the test changes the reaction area to the target state, click it as quickly as you can. The test records the elapsed time between the signal and your response.

After five rounds, Thinkora calculates your average and shows your fastest round along with the individual results.

What can affect reaction time?

Reaction time can change between attempts for several reasons. The result of an online test can also be influenced by the equipment and environment used during the test.

  • Attention: distractions can make it harder to respond immediately.
  • Fatigue: tiredness can affect alertness and consistency.
  • Practice: familiarity with the test can change your performance.
  • Input device: mouse and touchscreen input can introduce different delays.
  • Display: refresh behaviour and other display characteristics can affect online measurements.

How to get more consistent results

  • Use the same device when comparing different attempts.
  • Keep your hand ready near the mouse or input area.
  • Focus on the centre of the test area.
  • Reduce distractions while taking the test.
  • Compare results under similar lighting and device conditions.

Reaction time test vs IQ test

A reaction time test and an IQ test are different types of cognitive tasks. Reaction time testing focuses on responding quickly to a particular signal, while an IQ test can include multiple reasoning tasks such as numerical, logical and spatial problems.

A reaction time result should therefore not be interpreted as an IQ score. If you want to test several types of reasoning, you can try the free online IQ test.

Common questions

Is an online reaction time test accurate?

It can be useful for comparing your own results, but it is not the same as laboratory measurement. Display refresh, browser processing and input-device latency can affect the recorded time.

What is a good reaction time?

Many people produce results around 200 to 300 milliseconds on simple visual reaction tasks, but results vary depending on the task, device, attention, fatigue and testing conditions.

Why is my reaction time slower on my phone?

Touch input, display refresh and browser processing can introduce delays that vary between phones and computers.

Can reaction time improve with practice?

Practice can make you more familiar with a particular reaction-time task. Attention, fatigue, sleep and testing conditions can also affect your results.

What does ms mean in reaction time?

MS means milliseconds. One second contains 1,000 milliseconds, so milliseconds are used to describe the short response intervals measured by reaction time tests.
