    What is a good reaction time?

    For a simple visual reaction test like this one, many people score in the 200–300 millisecond range. Faster scores can be possible with practice and consistent equipment, while slower results can be affected by your screen, input device, attention and fatigue. Your result also includes the delay of your screen and mouse or touch input, so compare yourself with your own past scores as well as other results.

    How the test works

    Each round starts when you click the box. It turns coral while you wait, then switches to green after a random pause of one and a half to four seconds. The timer starts the moment green appears and stops when you click. If you click early, the round restarts. After five rounds you see your average and your best time.

    Tips for faster, fairer results

    • Use a mouse or keyboard on a computer for the most consistent times. Press Space or Enter if you prefer the keyboard.
    • Rest your finger on the button and look at the centre of the box instead of tracking it.
    • Play in a quiet spot, and ignore the first round if you were still getting ready.
    • Sleep, caffeine and stress can shift your time by a few tens of milliseconds.

    Common questions

    Is this test accurate?

    It is accurate enough to compare your own results over time. Screen refresh rate, browser and input device add a small delay, so it is not a lab measurement.

    Why is my time slower on my phone?

    Touch screens add input delay and the screen may refresh less often than a desktop monitor. Times can be slower on a phone because of differences in touch input, display refresh and browser processing.

    Can I improve my reaction time?

    A little. Practice, good sleep and staying focused can help. Reaction time also depends on factors such as attention, equipment and the type of task being tested.
