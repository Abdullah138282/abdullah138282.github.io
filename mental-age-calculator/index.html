What is mental age?

Mental age is the age at which an average child performs the same as the person being tested. Alfred Binet and Théodore Simon used the idea in their early intelligence scale in 1905: a child who solved the tasks that a typical eight-year-old could solve was said to have a mental age of eight, whatever their actual age was.

The mental age formula

The ratio between mental age and actual age was turned into the first IQ score. William Stern proposed it in 1912, and Lewis Terman popularized it in the Stanford–Binet test. The two formulas are the same relationship written in different ways:

  • IQ = mental age ÷ actual age × 100
  • Mental age = IQ ÷ 100 × actual age

An IQ of 100 always gives a mental age equal to actual age, because that is the average.

Why mental age does not work for adults

The formula assumes ability grows steadily with age, which is true for children but not for adults. If it were applied to a 40-year-old with an IQ of 120, it would give a mental age of 48, which does not describe anything real. Test publishers handled this by capping actual age at around 16 when using the ratio method, which is why the calculator also shows a capped result for anyone older. In practice, mental age stops being useful after adolescence.

Why modern IQ tests dropped it

Current tests compare your result with people of your own age and report a deviation IQ, with an average of 100. That method works the same way at every age and avoids the problems of the ratio formula. The mental age idea survives mostly in discussions of child development and in some disability assessments, where it is used as a rough description rather than a score.

Mental age is not how mature you are

People often search for a “mental age test” to find out how old they act. That is a different, informal idea. This calculator only converts between IQ, actual age and the classic test-based mental age. It is for information and learning, not for any clinical decision. For a formal assessment, see a licensed psychologist.

Common questions

How do you calculate mental age?

Multiply the person's IQ by their actual age and divide by 100. A 10-year-old with an IQ of 120 has a mental age of 12, because 120 ÷ 100 × 10 = 12.

What does mental age mean?

Mental age describes the age at which an average child performs the same way as the person tested. A mental age of 12 means the person performs like a typical 12-year-old on that test.

Is mental age the same as how mature I am?

No. Mental age here is a test-performance idea from early intelligence testing. It says nothing about emotional maturity, personality or how old you act or feel.

Does mental age work for adults?

Not well. The ratio formula was designed for children, whose abilities grow quickly with age. Adults do not keep gaining test performance every year, so the numbers stop making sense, and test manuals traditionally capped actual age at about 16.

Why do modern IQ tests not use mental age?

Modern tests, such as the Wechsler scales, compare your score with people of your own age and report a deviation IQ with an average of 100. This works for all ages and is more consistent than the old ratio method.
