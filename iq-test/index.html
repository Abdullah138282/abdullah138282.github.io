How the Thinkora IQ test works

The test has 30 questions in four groups: spatial reasoning (shapes and patterns), numerical reasoning (number series and word problems), logical reasoning (clues and deduction) and applied reasoning (everyday problem solving). Questions begin easy and get harder, and harder questions count for more points. Learn more about IQ scores and how they are commonly interpreted.

Your score is worked out in your browser the moment you finish. It is placed on the standard IQ scale, where the average is 100 and about two thirds of people score between 85 and 115. Wrong answers lose a small number of points, so random guessing does not inflate a result. You can also use our IQ percentile calculator to see how a score compares with the wider population.

Tips for a fair result

  • Take the test when you are rested and will not be interrupted.
  • Read each question fully before you look at the answers.
  • If a question takes more than a minute, skip it and return later.
  • Use the answer keys 1 to 4 and the arrow keys to move faster on a computer.
  • Treat the result as a snapshot. Sleep, stress and mood can move it by several points.

Common questions

How long does the Thinkora IQ test take?

The test has 30 questions and a 20-minute time limit. Most people finish in 15 to 20 minutes.

Is this IQ test accurate?

It gives an estimate, not a clinical diagnosis. Expect a margin of several points either way. For an official result, see a licensed psychologist.

Can I go back to a question?

Yes. Use the Back button to return to earlier questions and change your answers before you finish.

Does guessing help?

Wrong answers lose a small number of points, so random guessing does not raise your score. If you can rule out one or two options, a guess is reasonable. Otherwise you can skip.

What is an average IQ score?

Scores are set so that the average is 100, and about two thirds of people score between 85 and 115.
