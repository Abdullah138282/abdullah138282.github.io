Anagram speed test

How the anagram speed test works

You have 60 seconds. Each round shows a set of scrambled letters, and you type the word they spell. A correct answer loads the next puzzle straight away. Words start at four letters. After three solved words you get five-letter words, after seven you get six letters, and after twelve you get seven.

If a word will not come, press Skip. Skipping costs no time and no points, but it does not add to your score either. Your final score is the number of words you unscramble before the timer ends.

What does unscrambling words measure?

Unscrambling letters draws on vocabulary, pattern recognition and flexible thinking, because you have to drop one arrangement of the letters and try another. Your speed also depends on how fast you type and how familiar you are with common letter pairs.

This is a brain game, not a formal test of language ability or intelligence, so treat a single score as a bit of fun and a way to track your own practice.

Tips to unscramble words faster

  • Look for common endings such as ING, ER, ED and LY and set those letters aside first.
  • Group letter pairs that often sit together, like TH, CH, SH and ST.
  • Try different orders in your head instead of staring at the tiles. Saying the letters out loud can help.
  • Place the rarer letters first. Q is almost always followed by U, and J, K and Z often start a word.
  • Count the vowels. Most English words mix vowels and consonants, which helps you rule out arrangements quickly.
  • If a word has not come in about ten seconds, skip it. A fresh puzzle is usually faster than a stubborn one.

What is a good anagram score?

There is no standard score for this game. Results vary with vocabulary, typing speed and practice, and most people improve noticeably after a few attempts as they learn to spot letter patterns quickly.

Your own earlier attempts are the most useful comparison.

Anagram test vs IQ test

Unscrambling words exercises one narrow skill. A broader reasoning test covers several, which is why our free online IQ test includes a verbal reasoning section alongside shapes, numbers and logic. A word game should not be used as a replacement for an IQ assessment.

Common questions

Can one set of letters make more than one word?

Sometimes. The puzzles are chosen so that each has one common answer, and the game accepts any matching word from its own list. If a real word you typed is rejected, skip it and move on to the next puzzle.

Do I lose time or points for a wrong answer?

No. A wrong answer does not remove time from the 60-second timer and does not reduce your score. It simply does not count as solved.

Do I need to type in capital letters?

No. Capital and lowercase letters both work, so type however is quickest for you.

Is my anagram score saved?

Your best score is kept on the page for the current visit only. It is not saved as a permanent account score.
