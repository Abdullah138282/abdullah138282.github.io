How is IQ calculated?

Updated October 1, 2026  ·  5 min read

Key takeaways

  • An IQ score is not simply the number of questions answered correctly. Test performance is interpreted against a reference or norm group.
  • Modern IQ tests commonly use a standardized scale with an average of 100 and a standard deviation of 15.
  • The position of a score within the reference distribution can also be expressed as an IQ percentile.
  • Different tests can produce different scores because they use different tasks, norms and scoring procedures.

How is IQ calculated?

IQ is calculated by comparing performance on a standardized intelligence test with the performance of a reference group. The resulting position is converted to a standardized IQ score. On the commonly used scale, the average is 100 and the standard deviation is 15.

In simple terms, the process goes from a person's test performance to a raw score, then to a standardized score, and finally to a percentile that shows how the result compares with the reference population.

Step 1: Calculate the raw test score

An IQ test may contain questions involving pattern recognition, verbal reasoning, numerical reasoning, working memory, visual reasoning or other cognitive tasks.

The first result is generally a raw score. This can be the number of questions answered correctly or, depending on the assessment, a weighted score across different sections.

A raw score by itself is not an IQ score. For example, getting 30 questions correct does not have the same meaning on every test. The difficulty of the questions and the test's scoring system affect what that raw performance represents.

Step 2: Compare the score with a norm group

Test developers use a large sample of people to establish norms for the assessment. This group is often called a norm group or standardization sample.

The norm group helps show how common different levels of performance are. Depending on the assessment, comparisons can account for factors such as age and other characteristics relevant to the test's norms.

This comparison is important because the same raw score can have different meanings on different assessments. An IQ score therefore represents relative performance rather than simply counting correct answers.

Step 3: Convert performance to an IQ score

After a person's position in the reference distribution is established, it can be expressed as a standardized score. A widely used IQ scale has a mean of 100 and a standard deviation of 15.

A simplified way to represent this conversion is with a z-score:

IQ = 100 + 15 × z

Here, z represents how far a person's performance is from the reference-group average in standard-deviation units.

For example, a z-score of 0 corresponds to an IQ of 100 on this scale. A z-score of 1 corresponds to 115, while a z-score of 2 corresponds to 130.

What is a deviation IQ?

Modern IQ tests generally use what is known as a deviation IQ. Instead of calculating intelligence as a simple ratio of mental age to chronological age, the score represents a person's position within a standardized distribution.

This approach makes it possible to compare a person's performance with an appropriate reference group while keeping the scale centered around a defined average.

How was IQ calculated in the past?

Early intelligence testing used a different approach sometimes called the ratio IQ. It involved a ratio between estimated mental age and chronological age, multiplied by 100.

The historical formula can be represented as:

IQ = (mental age ÷ chronological age) × 100

Modern intelligence tests generally do not calculate adult IQ this way. Contemporary scoring instead relies on standardized norms and deviation scores.

How does IQ convert to a percentile?

Once an IQ score is placed on its reference distribution, it can also be described using a percentile. A percentile indicates the percentage of the reference population scoring below a particular result.

On a standard scale with a mean of 100 and a standard deviation of 15, some approximate examples are:

Approximate IQ percentiles on a standard scale
IQ score Approximate percentile Interpretation
85 16th Higher than about 16% of the reference group
100 50th Near the reference-group average
110 75th Higher than about 75% of the reference group
115 84th Higher than about 84% of the reference group
120 91st Higher than about 91% of the reference group
130 98th Higher than about 98% of the reference group

Percentiles are useful because they put an IQ score into relative context. You can use the IQ percentile calculator to convert an IQ score into an approximate percentile.

Why does the same person get different IQ scores?

IQ scores are not perfectly interchangeable between tests. Different assessments can use different questions, cognitive tasks, norm groups, scoring methods and standard deviations.

  • Different tests: Each assessment may measure cognitive abilities in somewhat different ways.
  • Different norm groups: Tests can use different samples and standardization periods.
  • Different scoring systems: Not every assessment uses exactly the same mean or standard deviation.
  • Testing conditions: Attention, fatigue, familiarity and other testing conditions can influence performance.
  • Measurement error: A test score is an estimate and should not be interpreted as an infinitely precise number.

Does the number of correct answers determine IQ?

Not by itself. The number of correct answers contributes to the test result, but an IQ score depends on how that performance is interpreted using the test's scoring and normative system.

This is why there is no universal formula such as “X correct answers equals an IQ of Y” that works across all IQ tests.

What does an IQ score actually tell you?

An IQ score describes performance relative to a reference population on the abilities measured by a particular test. It can provide useful information about certain types of cognitive performance, but it is not a complete description of a person's abilities or potential.

Frequently asked questions

How is IQ calculated?

IQ is calculated by comparing performance on a standardized test with a reference or norm group and converting the result into a standardized score. A commonly used scale has an average of 100 and a standard deviation of 15.

Is IQ based on the number of questions you get right?

The number of correct answers contributes to a test's raw score, but raw scores are interpreted using the test's scoring system and reference norms. There is no single correct-answer-to-IQ formula that applies to every test.

What is the average IQ?

The average IQ is 100 on the commonly used standardized IQ scale. The exact scoring system can vary between assessments.

What is a z-score in IQ testing?

A z-score describes how far a result is from the reference-group average in standard-deviation units. On a scale with a mean of 100 and standard deviation of 15, the simplified relationship is IQ = 100 + 15 × z.
