How is IQ calculated?

Updated October 1, 2026  ·  5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Your IQ is not the number of questions you get right. It is how your result compares with a reference group.
  • Modern tests use a deviation IQ: the average is 100 and the standard deviation is usually 15.
  • Percentiles come from the bell curve, so every IQ score maps to a rank.

Step 1: the raw score

An IQ test is made up of tasks such as pattern puzzles, vocabulary, number series and memory exercises. Your first result is a raw score: the number of items you answered correctly, or a weighted total across sections. On its own, a raw score of 32 means very little, because it depends on how hard the items were.

Step 2: compare with a norm group

Test makers give the test to a large sample of people, called the norm group or standardisation sample. A good sample matches the population in age, sex, region, education and other factors. The distribution of raw scores in this group tells you what is typical for each age.

Your raw score is then compared with people of your own age. This is why a child and an adult can have the same IQ with very different raw scores.

Step 3: convert to the standard scale

Your position in the norm group is converted to a number on a scale where the average is 100 and the standard deviation is 15. This is called a deviation IQ, and the idea is credited to the psychologist David Wechsler. The conversion uses a z-score:

IQ = 100 + 15 × z

A z-score of 0 means you scored exactly at the average, so your IQ is 100. A z-score of 1 means you scored one standard deviation above average, so your IQ is 115. A z-score of 2 gives 130.

What about “mental age”?

Early IQ tests used a ratio: mental age divided by actual age, multiplied by 100. The term “intelligence quotient” comes from this idea. The ratio method works poorly for adults, because mental age stops rising while actual age keeps growing, so modern tests use the deviation method instead.

From IQ to percentile

Because scores follow a bell curve, every IQ maps to a rank. An IQ of 115 is about the 84th percentile, 130 is about the 98th, and 70 is about the 2nd. You can try any score in the IQ percentile calculator, which also supports the SD 16 and SD 24 scales that some tests use.

Why scores differ between tests

  • Different scales. Not every test uses a standard deviation of 15.
  • Different norm groups. A test normed on a different population or in a different decade gives different results.
  • Different skills. One test may lean on verbal ability, another on visual puzzles.
  • Measurement error. Every test has a margin of error of a few points.
