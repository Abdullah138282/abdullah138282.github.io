Are online IQ tests accurate?

Updated October 1, 2026  ·  5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Online tests give an estimate. They are not a replacement for a professional assessment.
  • The biggest limits are the norm group, the testing conditions and the short length.
  • Use your result to compare ranges, not to claim an exact number.

The honest answer

Free online IQ tests can give you a rough idea of where you sit, but they are not accurate enough to give you an exact IQ. A clinical test such as a Wechsler scale is given one to one by a trained psychologist, takes more than an hour and is built from years of research with thousands of people. A web test cannot copy that.

What makes a test reliable

  • A large, representative norm group. The test is compared with people who match the population, so a score means the same thing for everyone.
  • Many items across several skills. More questions reduce the effect of luck and of any one weak area.
  • Controlled conditions. Same instructions, same timing and no outside help.
  • Published evidence. Studies that check the test measures what it says, and that scores are stable on a retest.

Where online tests fall short

Short length. A 20 to 40 question test samples far less than a professional battery, so scores swing more.

Uncontrolled setting. Noise, a small screen, a tired mind or a quick peek at a search engine can all change the result.

Weak norming. Many online tests have never been compared with a large, balanced sample. Some simply flatter the user, because happy users share and return.

Practice effects. Repeating the same test a few times raises the score without raising ability.

What an online test is good for

Used sensibly, a free test is a fun way to meet the kinds of questions IQ tests use, to see roughly which band you fall in, and to get curious about how thinking works. If you take the same test twice, a big jump or drop tells you more about the test’s noise than about you.

Warning signs of a poor test

  • It promises to reveal your “true IQ” or a genius-level score.
  • It asks for payment to see the result you already earned.
  • It never says how scores are worked out.
  • Everyone seems to get a flattering number.

How to read your result

Look at the band, not the exact figure. Whether you got 112 or 118, the honest message is “somewhat above average”. Put your number into the percentile calculator to see the rank, and read the score guide for what each range means. We describe Thinkora scores as estimates for exactly this reason. If you need a formal result, for example for school support or a high-IQ society, ask a qualified psychologist for a full assessment.
