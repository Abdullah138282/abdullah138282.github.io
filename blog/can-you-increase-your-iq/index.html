Can you increase your IQ?

Updated October 1, 2026  ·  6 min read

Key takeaways

  • IQ scores can change over time, although they tend to become more stable during adulthood.
  • Education has some of the strongest evidence for influencing performance on cognitive tests.
  • Sleep, physical activity, learning and healthy habits can support cognitive performance, but they are not guaranteed methods for permanently increasing IQ.
  • Practising similar IQ tests can improve test performance through familiarity without necessarily producing the same change in general cognitive ability.

Can you increase your IQ?

IQ scores can change, but there is no simple method that reliably produces a large, permanent increase for everyone. Cognitive test performance can be influenced by education, age, health, environment, familiarity with testing and the particular assessment being taken.

IQ is therefore not completely fixed across a person's lifetime. At the same time, a change in an IQ score does not automatically mean that a person's general cognitive ability changed by exactly the same amount.

Research on the Flynn effect also shows that average performance on intelligence tests can change substantially across generations.

How stable is IQ?

IQ scores generally become more stable as people get older. In adulthood, a person's relative position on cognitive tests tends to be fairly consistent, but scores can still vary because of development, education, health, life circumstances, measurement error and differences between tests.

Different IQ tests also measure somewhat different combinations of cognitive abilities. A test may place greater emphasis on reasoning, verbal ability, working memory, processing speed or visual problem solving.

Can education increase IQ?

Education is one of the factors with substantial evidence linking additional schooling with higher performance on cognitive tests. Research has also suggested that part of this relationship may reflect an effect of education itself rather than only differences between people who choose to remain in education.

However, the size of the effect varies between studies, populations, ages and types of cognitive tests. Education should not be treated as a guaranteed way to add a specific number of IQ points to every person.

Can brain training increase IQ?

Brain-training games can improve performance on the particular tasks that you practise. The more difficult question is whether those improvements transfer broadly to unrelated cognitive abilities or produce a substantial increase in general intelligence.

Evidence is generally stronger for improvement on trained tasks than for large, general increases in cognitive ability. In other words, becoming better at a memory game does not automatically mean that your IQ has increased.

Brain games can still be useful for practice and entertainment. Thinkora offers several activities, including a number memory test, pattern memory game and reaction time test.

Can sleep improve IQ?

Sleep plays an important role in attention, learning and memory. Poor sleep can temporarily affect concentration and problem solving, which can also influence how someone performs on a cognitive test.

Getting adequate sleep is therefore useful for cognitive performance, but it is more accurate to describe good sleep as supporting normal performance than as a guaranteed method for permanently raising IQ.

Can exercise increase IQ?

Regular physical activity is associated with physical and cognitive health. Exercise can support overall well-being and may benefit aspects of cognitive functioning, but it should not be presented as a guaranteed IQ-boosting technique.

A healthy lifestyle can help you perform closer to your current abilities, especially when factors such as poor sleep, inactivity or fatigue would otherwise interfere with concentration.

Can reading and learning improve intelligence?

Learning challenging subjects can develop useful knowledge and skills. Reading can strengthen vocabulary and background knowledge, while learning a language, musical instrument, mathematics or technical subject can provide sustained cognitive practice.

These activities are valuable even when they do not produce a measurable increase in overall IQ. Cognitive ability and useful real-world skills are broader than a single standardized score.

Can practising IQ tests raise your score?

Practising similar IQ tests can raise your score on later attempts. This can happen because you become familiar with the question formats, timing, instructions and strategies used by the test.

This is known as a practice effect. A higher score after repeated exposure does not necessarily represent an equally large change in general cognitive ability.

For this reason, repeatedly taking the same or very similar IQ test is not a reliable way to determine whether your underlying ability has changed.

What can temporarily lower your IQ test score?

Your performance on a cognitive test can be affected by conditions that have little to do with a permanent change in ability.

  • Fatigue: being tired can make sustained attention and problem solving more difficult.
  • Poor sleep: insufficient sleep can affect attention, memory and concentration.
  • Distractions: noise, interruptions and an uncomfortable environment can interfere with performance.
  • Stress or anxiety: emotional pressure can affect concentration and test-taking performance.
  • Unfamiliarity: someone unfamiliar with a test format may perform differently from someone who has practised similar questions.

What is the best way to support cognitive performance?

There is no single shortcut for permanently raising IQ. A more useful approach is to build habits that support learning and cognitive performance over time.

  • Get adequate and consistent sleep.
  • Stay physically active.
  • Continue learning challenging subjects and skills.
  • Read and build knowledge in areas that interest you.
  • Practise problem solving rather than relying only on passive consumption of information.
  • Take cognitive tests under appropriate conditions when you want to compare your performance.

Does a higher IQ score always mean you became smarter?

Not necessarily. A change in test score can reflect genuine changes in cognitive performance, but it can also reflect differences in the test, practice effects, testing conditions, health, education or measurement uncertainty.

This is why a single IQ number should not be treated as a complete description of someone's abilities.

What about the Flynn effect?

The Flynn effect provides an important example of how intelligence test performance can change across generations. Average performance on many intelligence tests increased across much of the 20th century in numerous populations, although the size of the change differed across countries and tests.

The pattern also changed in some populations during later periods. This demonstrates why cognitive test scores should be interpreted in the context of the population, test and norms used to produce them.

Can you increase your IQ permanently?

There is no universally reliable strategy that can be presented as a guaranteed way to permanently increase IQ by a specific number of points.

Cognitive development is influenced by many factors, and some changes in cognitive test performance can be meaningful while others may reflect temporary conditions or familiarity with the assessment.

A better long-term goal is to develop useful cognitive skills, knowledge and healthy habits rather than focusing entirely on changing one number.

Think beyond one score

IQ is one standardized measure of cognitive performance. Learning, memory, concentration, reasoning, knowledge and problem-solving skills all matter in different situations. Improving these abilities can be valuable even if your measured IQ remains relatively stable.

Frequently asked questions

Can you increase your IQ?

IQ scores can change, but there is no simple method that reliably produces a large permanent increase for everyone. Education, development, health, environment and testing factors can all influence cognitive test performance.

Can adults increase their IQ?

Adult IQ scores tend to be relatively stable, but they can still change. Education, learning, health, life circumstances, practice effects and differences between tests can influence measured performance.

Do brain games increase IQ?

Brain games can improve performance on the tasks that are practised. Evidence for a broad increase in general intelligence is more limited, so a better score on a particular game should not automatically be interpreted as a higher IQ.

Can studying increase IQ?

Education and additional schooling are associated with higher performance on cognitive tests, and research suggests that education can have a causal role in some cognitive outcomes. The size of any effect varies between people and studies.

Does practising IQ tests increase IQ?

Practising similar questions can improve performance on later tests because of familiarity and practice effects. That does not necessarily mean that general intelligence increased by the same amount.

Can sleep make your IQ higher?

Good sleep supports attention, learning and memory and can help you perform closer to your current ability. It should not be described as a guaranteed way to permanently increase IQ.
