What is a good IQ score?

Updated October 1, 2026  ·  5 min read

Key takeaways

  • 100 is average by design, and about half of people score between 90 and 109.
  • 110 or higher is above average (about 1 in 4 people). 130 or higher is about 1 in 44.
  • A single score has a margin of error, so a few points either way is normal.

The short answer

An IQ score of 100 is average, and anything from 90 to 109 counts as average too. A score of 110 or higher is above average, 120 or higher is superior, and 130 or higher puts you in roughly the top 2% of people. Whether a score is “good” depends on what you are comparing it with and what you plan to use it for.

What the numbers mean

IQ tests are built so that scores form a bell curve with an average of 100 and a standard deviation of 15. That gives some easy landmarks:

  • About 68 in 100 people score between 85 and 115.
  • About 95 in 100 score between 70 and 130.
  • About 2 in 100 score above 130, and about 2 in 100 score below 70.

Is 120 a good IQ? Is 130?

Yes, both are clearly above average. A score of 120 is higher than about 91 out of 100 people, which means roughly 1 in 11 people reach it. A score of 130 is about 1 in 44. Many universities and gifted programmes use 130 as an informal marker for high ability, but there is no single official line, and the rules vary from place to place.

Why a single number is not the whole picture

Even a professional IQ test has a margin of error of a few points. If you score 118 one day and 124 another, that does not mean you got smarter in a week. It is better to read a score as a range than as an exact value.

An IQ score also measures a narrow slice of thinking: reasoning, pattern recognition, working memory and processing speed. It does not measure creativity, curiosity, social skill, persistence or judgement, all of which matter a great deal in school, work and life.

Does the test matter?

Yes. A score from a clinical test given by a psychologist is far more reliable than one from a free online quiz. Online tests give an estimate, and results from different websites can differ by 10 points or more. If an exact number matters for a decision, such as school placement, ask a qualified professional for a formal assessment.

How to use your score

Treat your score as one piece of information, not a label. If you want to see what a number means in rank terms, type it into the IQ percentile calculator, and read the IQ score guide for every range in detail.
