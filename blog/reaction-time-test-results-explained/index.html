Reaction time test results explained

Updated October 1, 2026  ·  5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Most people score between 200 and 300 ms on a simple visual test.
  • Screen, device and browser add delay, so compare yourself with your own past results.
  • Reaction time is a narrow skill and does not measure IQ.

What is a good reaction time?

On a simple visual test, where you click as soon as the screen changes colour, most people land between 200 and 300 milliseconds (ms). Under 200 ms is quick. Over 300 ms is common on phones, slower screens, or when you are tired or distracted. One millisecond is a thousandth of a second, so the difference between 220 ms and 260 ms is about the blink of a fast eyelid.

What happens during those milliseconds

Your reaction time is the total of several steps: your eyes detect the change, your brain decides to act, a signal travels to your hand, and your finger presses the button. Part of this delay is nerve signal speed, which does not change much with practice. That is why the gains from training are small.

What adds delay that is not you

  • Screen refresh rate. A 60 Hz screen updates about every 17 ms, so the change can appear up to 17 ms late. Faster screens cut this.
  • Input device. Mice, keyboards and touch screens each add a different delay. Touch screens are often 20 to 50 ms slower than a desktop mouse.
  • Browser and system load. Many open tabs or a busy computer can add small lags.
  • Wireless devices. A cheap wireless mouse may be slower than a wired one.

Because of these, an online test is accurate enough to compare your own results over time but is not a lab measurement.

What changes your own reaction time

  • Age. Reaction times tend to be quickest in young adulthood and slow gradually with age.
  • Sleep. Lack of sleep slows reactions noticeably.
  • Attention. Distraction and stress make times slower and more varied.
  • Caffeine. It can shift your time a little for some people.
  • Practice. Time improves slightly in the first few tries, then levels off.

Tips for a fair result

  • Use the same device and browser each time.
  • Rest your finger on the button and look at the centre of the box.
  • Take several rounds and look at the average, not one lucky click.
  • Ignore your first round if you were still getting ready.

Does reaction time say anything about IQ?

Research has found a modest link between how quickly people respond on simple tasks and their scores on intelligence tests. The link is too weak to estimate someone’s IQ from a reaction time, and a slow time says nothing about how well you think. Treat it as one narrow skill. Test yours with the reaction time test, and for a broader look at your thinking try the IQ test.
