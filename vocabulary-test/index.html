    How the vocabulary test works

    The test has 20 questions in three levels. For each one you see a word and four possible meanings, and you choose the one that fits best. The first five words are everyday English, the next eight are intermediate, and the last seven are advanced.

    After every answer the test shows whether you were right, and if not, what the word means. At the end you see your score out of 20 and a list of the words you missed so you can review them.

    What does a vocabulary test measure?

    It measures how many English words you recognize and can match to a meaning. Vocabulary grows with reading, schooling and daily use of the language, so a score reflects your experience with English as much as anything else.

    It is not a test of speaking, writing or grammar, and it is not an IQ test. A short game like this gives a rough picture of your word knowledge, nothing more.

    Ways to build your vocabulary

    • Read widely. Articles, books and long-form writing show you words in real sentences.
    • Learn a new word together with a sentence that uses it, not on its own.
    • Keep a short list of new words and review it a few days later, then a week later.
    • Use a new word in speech or writing within a day of learning it.
    • Notice word parts. Knowing that “bene” points to good and “mal” to bad helps you guess unfamiliar words.

    What does your score mean?

    A score of 18 to 20 is labeled advanced vocabulary, 14 to 17 is strong, 9 to 13 is good everyday vocabulary, and below that is still growing. These labels describe this short game only. They are not an official grade or a measure of overall English ability.

    Vocabulary test vs IQ test

    Word knowledge is only one piece of verbal ability. Our free online IQ test includes eight verbal reasoning questions that mix word meaning with language logic, next to shapes, numbers and applied problems. A vocabulary quiz alone should not be used as an IQ assessment.

    Common questions

    Is the vocabulary test timed?

    No. Take as long as you like on each question. There is no timer, so the score reflects the words you know rather than how fast you click.

    Do the questions change every time?

    Yes. Each round picks words at random from a larger bank at three difficulty levels, so you will usually see a different mix when you try again.

    Is this a test of English proficiency?

    No. It is a short word-meaning game. It does not measure speaking, writing or grammar, and it is not a standardized English exam.

    Is my score saved?

    No. Your result is shown at the end of the round and is not stored as a permanent account score.
