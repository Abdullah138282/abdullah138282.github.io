IQ score chart and ranges explained

Understand IQ scores, ranges, classifications, percentiles and how rare different scores are.

Last updated October 1, 2026

What does an IQ score mean?

An IQ score is a standardized score that describes how a person's performance on a cognitive test compares with a reference population. It is not a direct measurement of every aspect of intelligence, ability or potential.

On the commonly used standard IQ scale, the mean score is 100 and the standard deviation is 15. This means the score is interpreted relative to a statistical distribution rather than as a percentage or number of questions answered correctly.

IQ score chart

IQ scores are commonly grouped into ranges to make results easier to describe. The classifications below are based on a commonly used interpretation of the standard scale with a mean of 100 and standard deviation of 15.

Classification labels can vary between tests and assessment systems. They should be used to describe test performance rather than as a complete description of a person.

IQ scores and percentiles

An IQ percentile describes the approximate position of a score within a reference population. For example, the 90th percentile means a score is higher than approximately 90% of the reference group.

IQ and percentile are different measurements. IQ is the standardized score, while percentile describes relative standing within a distribution.

For example, an IQ of 100 is at the 50th percentile on the standard normal model. An IQ of 120 is approximately at the 91st percentile, while an IQ of 130 is approximately at the 98th percentile.

How rare is a high IQ score?

On a standard scale with a mean of 100 and standard deviation of 15, scores become less common as they move farther from the average.

These figures are theoretical approximations based on a normal distribution with a mean of 100 and standard deviation of 15. They should not be interpreted as exact counts of people in the real world.

Extremely high scores can also be harder to estimate precisely because relatively few people are represented in the highest part of a test's norming sample.

What is the average IQ?

The average IQ is 100 on the standard IQ scale. Approximately two-thirds of people fall between 85 and 115 when the scores follow a normal distribution with a standard deviation of 15.

A score above 100 is above the mean, while a score below 100 is below the mean. The distance from 100 matters because the same numerical difference can represent different percentile changes at different points of the distribution.

The exact interpretation of an IQ score depends on the particular test, its norms, scoring system, reference population and standard deviation.

What do IQ scores like 100, 120, 130 and 140 mean?

On the standard scale:

  • IQ 100: The mean or average score.
  • IQ 110: Above the mean and approximately at the 75th percentile.
  • IQ 120: Approximately at the 91st percentile.
  • IQ 130: Approximately at the 98th percentile.
  • IQ 140: Approximately at the 99.6th percentile.

Percentiles shown here are approximate because they are based on the standard normal model rather than a specific test's actual norming sample.

Why can the same person get different IQ scores?

IQ scores are estimates, and different assessments can produce somewhat different results. Several factors can contribute to variation.

  • Different tests: Tests can measure different cognitive abilities and use different scoring and norming systems.
  • Measurement error: No psychological measurement produces a perfectly precise number.
  • Testing conditions: Sleep, stress, distractions, motivation and concentration can influence performance.
  • Practice and familiarity: Previous experience with similar questions or test formats can affect performance.
  • Norms and populations: Scores are interpreted against the reference population and norms used by the particular assessment.

This is one reason an online IQ test should generally be treated as an estimate rather than as a substitute for a professionally administered assessment.

See our article about whether online IQ tests are accurate for more information about online testing and its limitations.

Can IQ scores change over time?

IQ scores can vary between assessments and testing sessions. Some variation is expected because tests contain measurement error and because performance can change with testing conditions.

Researchers also study how education, environment, development and other factors relate to changes in cognitive test performance over time.

The Flynn effect is one example of research into changes in average cognitive test scores across generations and why tests may need to be periodically re-normed.

What an IQ score does not tell you

  • It does not measure every type of intelligence or ability.
  • It does not directly measure creativity, motivation, personality, wisdom or emotional skills.
  • It does not determine how well someone will perform in every job, subject or situation.
  • It cannot by itself diagnose a medical, psychological or learning condition.

Frequently asked questions about IQ scores

What is a normal IQ score?

Scores from 90 to 109 are classified as average in the range shown on this page. On a standard scale with a mean of 100 and standard deviation of 15, scores from 85 to 115 cover roughly two-thirds of the population.

What is a good IQ score?

A score of 110 or above is above average on the standard scale. An IQ of 120 is approximately at the 91st percentile. What a score means depends on the test and purpose of the assessment. See what is a good IQ score for more detail.

What IQ score is considered genius?

There is no universally accepted IQ cutoff for the word "genius." The term is often used informally for very high scores, sometimes around 140 or above. It is not a formal classification used consistently across all IQ tests.

Is an IQ of 100 average?

Yes. An IQ of 100 is the mean score on the standard IQ scale. The scale is designed around an average of 100 and a standard deviation of 15.

Does IQ change over time?

IQ scores can vary between tests and sessions. Measurement error, sleep, motivation, stress, familiarity with testing and other factors can contribute to differences.
